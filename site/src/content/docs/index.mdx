<p class="ek-kicker">Why</p>

## Without evals, your users find out first

<div class="ek-lede">
  An AI feature can get worse without a single line of your code changing.
  Trying a few prompts by hand won't catch it, and it won't tell you whether a
  cheaper model is good enough.
</div>

<div class="ek-panels">
  <div>
    <strong>The model changes under you</strong>
    <p>
      Providers retire models, update them under the same name, and change
      prices. A feature that worked in March can quietly slip by June.
    </p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <strong>A fix in one place breaks another</strong>
    <p>
      In one app, a prompt rule cut small misses from 8 to 5 and raised made-up
      details from 4 to 7. Nobody saw it until a test did.
    </p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <strong>The cheaper model might do</strong>
    <p>
      A model at half the price ships. Is it good enough for this feature? Ten
      tries in a playground can't tell you.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

</section>

<section class="ek-section">

<p class="ek-kicker">New to evals?</p>

## An eval is a test for an AI feature

<div class="ek-lede">
  Like a unit test, but for answers that can vary. You collect real examples,
  write down what a good answer is, and grade every model against them.
  Evalkeeper does the bookkeeping, in pages inside your own admin.
</div>

<div class="ek-panels">
  <div>
    <span class="ek-step">1</span>
    <strong>Collect real examples</strong>
    <p>
      Inputs your feature really sees: a support ticket, a meeting's notes. Each
      one is a case.
    </p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <span class="ek-step">2</span>
    <strong>Confirm the right answers</strong>
    <p>
      A model can draft them, but a person on your team confirms each one.
      Together they're your answer key.
    </p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <span class="ek-step">3</span>
    <strong>Test every model</strong>
    <p>
      Run each model on every case and grade its answers, with what each one
      costs and how long it takes.
    </p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <span class="ek-step">4</span>
    <strong>Choose on evidence</strong>
    <p>
      Make the best model live, keep a fallback, and see every change on the
      record, with the test behind it.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<p class="ek-kicker">How it fits</p>

## Four steps to your first test

<div class="ek-lede">
  A small library, not a platform. Your app keeps its own routing, auth, and
  pages, and Evalkeeper fits around what you already have.
</div>

<Steps>

1. **Name your AI calls.** Route each one through an operation, so a
   feature's model can change without a deploy.

2. **Add the tables.** `npx @crowandraven/evalkeeper migration` writes
   Evalkeeper's models beside your Prisma schema, with the SQL. It never
   touches a database.

3. **Write a suite** for one feature: its input, what a good answer is, and
   the checks that grade one.

4. **Add the pages.** A few one-line route files, and the hub, the model
   board, and the answer key are in your admin.

</Steps>
